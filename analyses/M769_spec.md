# M769 spec: occupant payload (seatbelt states) and the fuel, battery-charge and traction-energy figures (report-only)

Rev 1, 2026-11-24. Committed BEFORE any tool is written or run. Author: main session (Sonnet). Owner request (Andrii, chat, 2026-11-24, item 2 of his answers): "Seatbelt study: Let's analyze the mass influence on Fuel consumption, battery charge and traction energy distribution". Parents: `analyses/M753_spec.md` (Rev 2; payload table `analyses/M753_drive_payload.csv`, 586 of 717 drives with a belt pattern, owner-estimated seat weights), `fuel_recon_master.csv` (per-drive fuel and energy-flow reconstruction), M768 (energy flows by estimated grade class, report-only). Next step: Director review, then tool and known-answer tests, blind audit, Director decision, CHANGELOG, PR. No payload, dashboard or published-figure change in this milestone; the class mass assumption is not replaced.

## 0. What is asked, and what can be answered
Two different questions, kept apart:
- **Q-A (association, drive level):** do drives with a larger occupant payload show higher fuel use (L/100 km, app-calculated), higher generator and traction energy per 100 km, and a different split between generator-to-traction, battery-to-traction and regeneration-to-battery energy? Observational; payload is an owner-estimated seat-weight proxy, not a measured mass; the drive-to-drive SD of payload (about 39 kg, 2 % of the car mass) is small, so the expected effect is small too.
- **Q-B (model-derived sensitivity of the displayed split to the assumed mass):** how would the reconstructed traction energy, its regeneration and generator parts change if the class mass were the occupant-informed mass (class mass + payload - allowance A, as in M753)? This is arithmetic on the reconstruction (mass-dependent terms scale with the mass; aerodynamic and auxiliary terms do not), labelled model-derived, bracketed, never a measurement.

## 1. Constraints
- Report-only. `drive_master.csv` MD5 d514d6009d6d9a0a168ad4fb09b9bb98 asserted before and after (a different master stops the tool, exit 3, nothing written; a `--retest` mode is out of scope). `fuel_recon_master.csv` and `analyses/M753_drive_payload.csv` are read-only inputs pinned by sha256. No winter-frozen file (M726). Outputs `analyses/M769_*`: aggregates, no coordinates, no per-drive rows in the public result (the per-drive payload table already committed in M753 is the only per-drive input).
- Language: fuel and rates are "logged / app-calculated" and "reconstructed / model-derived"; payload is "estimated from latched seatbelts with owner-estimated seat weights", never "measured mass"; a latched belt is not a weight. Never "effect of mass" for Q-A; "associated with". Q-B is "sensitivity of the reconstruction to the assumed mass".
- Statistics (CLAUDE.md): calendar day is the resampling cluster; day-clustered percentile bootstrap (seed 42, 4000 draws); every estimate carries n (drives, days) and a minimum detectable effect (MDE = 2.8 x bootstrap SD of the slope); the result states "not informative at this support" when the MDE exceeds the physically expected slope. No multiplicity claim: Q-A has ONE confirmatory estimand (below), the rest are exploratory.

## 2. Population and covariates
- Eligible: canonical-clean drives (`ens_outlier_v2` false) with a belt pattern (M753 `has_pattern`), fuel basis present in `fuel_recon_master.csv` for the fuel estimands, distance at least 5 km (short drives carry cold-start and rounding noise; a sensitivity at 2 km and at 10 km), speed class from the master (urban / mixed / highway). Counts reported per estimand: drives, days.
- Covariates for Q-A (all pre-existing columns, no new data): drive class (3 levels), ambient temperature (the drive ambient; thermal class cold / shoulder / warm as in the displayed cohorts), log distance, engine-on share where it is not an outcome. No grade covariate (owner rule: not in winter estimators; M768 treats grade separately).

## 3. Estimands
- **A1 (confirmatory):** the slope of fuel L/100 km on central payload (kg per 100 kg), adjusted for the covariates in section 2, day-clustered bootstrap interval, n drives and days, MDE. Physics expectation (stated before any result, as a pre-registered reference): fuel scales with the mass-dependent share of the road load; with a mass-dependent share of 0.6 to 0.8 of the traction energy, a payload of +100 kg of 1900 kg (+5.3 %) predicts about +3 to +4 % (about +0.2 L/100 km at the corpus mean). The reading rule: "informative" only if MDE <= half the expectation; else "not informative at this support", whatever the point estimate.
- **A2 (exploratory):** the same slope for generator energy, gross traction energy, battery-to-traction energy, regeneration-to-battery energy and gen-to-battery energy per 100 km, and for the shares of traction energy supplied by the generator, the battery and regeneration (the "distribution"); each with n, MDE and an interval; no verdict wording.
- **A3 (exploratory, support check):** the same slope within drive class (urban, mixed, highway) where the class has at least 100 drives and 30 days; counts only otherwise.
- **B1 (model-derived):** per drive, scale the mass-dependent terms of the reconstruction (inertial and rolling-resistance parts of traction; the regeneration potential, which is proportional to mass) by k_i = m_i(A) / m_class,i with A in {0, mean payload, 95th percentile} (M753 definitions) and the payload low / central / high bracket; the aerodynamic and auxiliary terms stay. Report the corpus aggregate relative change of gross traction energy per 100 km, regeneration-to-battery energy and the shares above, with a day-clustered interval over drives. The mass-dependent share is an explicit parameter in {0.6, 0.7, 0.8} (stated as an assumption: no road-load data exist, owner has none); no value is picked as "the" answer.
- **C (integrity):** payload coverage by month and class; the drives without a pattern compared with those with one on class, temperature and distance (selection check); the fraction of drives with a payload above the class allowance.

## 4. Falsification and acceptance
- A1 is falsified as "informative" when the MDE exceeds half the expectation; that outcome is a legitimate result, reported as such. The tool is accepted when its known-answer tests pass: a planted slope in a synthetic drive set is recovered inside its interval, the cluster bootstrap covers it, the MDE is computed, B1 with k = 1 reproduces the published reconstruction exactly (zero change), a different master stops, no per-drive row is written, and a planted covariate confound is removed by the adjustment.
- Control (R1): B1 at k = 1 equals `fuel_recon_master.csv` to the stored precision for every eligible drive.
- Placebo (R2): the same slope against a payload permuted across drives within day and drive class (1000 permutations): its interval must contain zero and its absolute value must be below the primary's in at least 95 % of permutations; otherwise A1 is reported as "association not separable from route and day structure".
