## Rulings
1. **No standing wording change** for either slope. No single interval is named primary (the spec did not pre-register one; choosing now would be selection by result): the released CR1 interval is the reference, then CR2, wild percentile-t, day pairs and week-block pairs in the spec's fixed order, formatted identically, without an envelope. The week-block interval may be labelled "dependence-conservative" because the reason was known in advance (residual day-to-day autocorrelation), not because of the result.
2. **Minimum effect with 80 % power** is acceptable as the MDE-type quantity beside "trend not resolved" (it is a power calculation, unlike the old half-width leaves); it must not stand beside "equivalence not established" (the TOST comparator remains the 90 % interval against delta). The standard error it rests on is itself an underestimate if dependence is present.
3. **Language gate:** W3-mde stays unchanged ("MDE" stays forbidden); new rule `W3-power80` forbids "observed power ...", "post hoc power", "powered to detect" and "rules out slopes"; the payload label uses "probability 0.8 at the observed standard error; design-conditional".
4. **KPI disclosure:** week-block intervals are about 1.6-2.1x wider for drive medians and 1.03-1.16x for corpus ratios (generated into the block). The intervals describe precision; they do not test the thesis, which may cite them only with "consistent with", never "demonstrates", and with K2 corrected and raw, throughput "integrated from logged current, estimated", and the M366 raw label. Article use still needs the Opus audit and Andrii's sign-off.
5. **Over-reads to avoid:** the slow-regime spread slope excluding 0 is subgroup selection and multiplicity, not a finding; the fast-regime week interval (6 blocks) is not interpretable; a wild p of 0.049 against the auditor's 0.040 is Monte Carlo noise.
