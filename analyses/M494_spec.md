# M494 spec (Rev 1): week-block and moving-block sensitivity for the Warm-vs-Shoulder urban contrast

Status: Rev 1, written 2026-10-23 BEFORE any M494 computation. Origin: external audit Update 7 (2026-10-22), page 'Highway versus city': 'A block sensitivity across neighboring dates is also warranted if the target is replication over time' (the joint-day bootstrap of M491 resamples calendar days; neighbouring days share weather, traffic and route mix). Additive and reporting-only: no released estimator, interval or decision changes. Needs: this spec, a Director ruling on the questions below, a blind audit, a Director decision on the result.

## Question
The released contrast (Shoulder minus Warm urban energy intensity, kWh/100 km, ratio of sums, four trip-length bands) has a joint calendar-day interval (M491). Days are not independent: neighbouring days are serially correlated, so a day-level resampling can be too narrow if the interest is replication over time. How wide is the interval, and does the reading ('includes zero' / 'excludes zero') change, when the resampling unit is a block of neighbouring dates?

## Estimand and population (unchanged, frozen)
Per band (all urban trips; < 3 km; 3-10 km; >= 10 km): difference = ratio of sums (Shoulder) minus ratio of sums (Warm), 100 x sum(gross_throughput_kwh) / sum(distance_km) per cohort, on canonical-clean urban drives with a gross energy integral and distance > 0, thermal regime warm or shoulder. The tool reproduces the cube's eligibility (ens_invalid / ens_outlier_v2 excluded, drive_type 'city' read as 'urban', distance > 0) and HARD-STOPS unless its point estimates, n drives and n days equal `comparisonCube.urbanContrast.bands[*]` exactly.

## Procedures (frozen; project conventions: calendar-day cluster, seed 42, 4,000 draws, percentile interval)
Unit axis: per calendar date, the Warm sums and the Shoulder sums (numerator kWh, denominator km) are formed; a date without a drive of a cohort contributes zero to that cohort; both cohorts are resampled TOGETHER (a drawn unit applies to both), as in the M491 primary.
- **W (primary sensitivity): ISO-calendar-week blocks.** Units = ISO weeks (`dependence_sensitivity.week_blocks`) of the union of dates of the band; weeks resampled with replacement (multinomial weights, `dependence_sensitivity.boot_weights`, seed 42, 4,000 draws); per draw the two ratios of sums and their difference.
- **M7 and M14 (secondary): overlapping moving blocks of 7 and 14 consecutive calendar days** on the calendar axis from the first to the last date of the band (days without a drive are empty), `ceil(D / L)` block starts drawn uniformly (the `serial_dependence.moving_block` construction, seed 42, 4,000 draws), per-day weights = block coverage; per draw the difference of ratios.
- A draw in which a cohort has a zero distance denominator is discarded and counted (cap 0.5 % of the draws per band and procedure: above it the milestone STOPS and goes to the Director).
- Few blocks: a band with fewer than 10 week units (union), or fewer than 10 moving-block starts' worth of calendar span (D < 10 L), is reported with the point estimate and `fewBlocks = true` and NO interval for that procedure (the percentile bootstrap is not defensible with so few units); nothing is tuned to obtain an interval.
- The M491 intervals (joint-day, independent-cohort, stratified) are read from the payload and shown beside, never recomputed here.

## Block content and display (reporting only)
Additive payload key `urbanContrastBlocks` (+ artifact stamp) written by `tools/urban_week_block.py` (ingestion stage after `comparison_cube`): per band the point estimate and counts, W, M7, M14 intervals with draws used / dropped, the number of blocks, the width ratio of each block interval to the joint-day interval, and 'includes zero' per procedure. The dashboard (Highway vs city, urban contrast) gets one sentence bound to the key: 'Block sensitivities (ISO calendar weeks; 7- and 14-day moving blocks): ...' with the verdict sentence below; the displayed interval stays the M491 joint-day interval.
Verdict rule (frozen): `unchanged` if every procedure with an interval includes zero in every band (or excludes zero in the same bands as the joint-day interval); `depends on the resampling unit` if any procedure differs from the joint-day reading in any band. The second verdict is reported as a sensitivity, never as a finding about the vehicle, and goes to the Director before the dashboard sentence is spliced.

## Known-answer tests (written before the run, `tests/synthetic/test_m494_block_bootstrap.py`)
- KAT1 serially independent days (>= 100 days, iid shocks): the mean width ratio W / joint-day over 20 seeds within 0.85-1.20 (blocks of one week of independent days barely change the width).
- KAT2 AR(1) day shocks with phi = 0.7 shared by both cohorts' ratios (>= 100 days): the mean width ratio W / joint-day over 20 seeds > 1.25, the same for M7.
- KAT3 reduction: with every date in its own block (block length 1) the moving-block weights equal multinomial day weights, and the W construction on data where each week holds one date reproduces the day-level interval within Monte Carlo error (mean over 20 seeds within 0.9-1.1).
- KAT4 hand-computed case: two weeks, known sums: the draws are the three possible two-week multisets and the ratios equal the hand values; the cap and few-block rules trigger on constructed inputs.
- KAT5 the hard stop: a modified cube point estimate makes the tool refuse.

F. Language: 'block sensitivity', 'uncertainty analysis, not a change of the point estimate'; the cohort label is confounded with season and day; never 'robust', 'confirmed', 'replicated' or 'no seasonal effect'.

## What would falsify the change
(i) points or counts differ from the cube; (ii) the independent-days KAT shows the block procedures materially wider on independent data (the construction would then inflate by itself); (iii) the AR(1) KAT shows no widening (the block construction would not capture serial dependence); (iv) draws dropped above the cap.

## Rev 2 (Director ruling 2026-10-23, decision 'revise'; written BEFORE any M494 computation)
Answers: (1) ISO weeks (W) primary, moving blocks M7 / M14 secondary; the moving blocks must be CIRCULAR (block starts uniform over all D positions, blocks wrap around the calendar axis). The non-circular start rule of `serial_dependence.moving_block` (starts in [0, D - L]) under-weights the first and last L - 1 days, which is where Warm (early) and Shoulder (late) sit, so the bootstrap distribution would be off-centre; the tool therefore reimplements the construction for ratios of sums (stated here; the released moving-block construction is not changed). (2) Seed 42 / 4,000 draws for the new tool; the deviation from the cube's registered seed 20260925 (streams SEED+2, SEED+3) is recorded. (3) Few blocks: the interval is withheld (point estimate and `fewBlocks = true`); no hidden interval is stored. (4) No Cold block analysis (M465). (5) A `depends on the resampling unit` verdict stops before the splice; a band in which every procedure is `fewBlocks` gets the verdict 'not assessable', never 'unchanged'.
Amendments (frozen; they replace conflicting text above):
- A1. Stratified sensitivity WS: strata = warm-only, shoulder-only and mixed ISO weeks (a week is mixed if both cohorts have a drive in it), resampled with replacement within each stratum, draws per stratum = stratum size (the M491 stratified analogue). The union design randomises how many weeks each cohort gets because the regime is an ambient-threshold class clustered in time. The verdict rule covers WS.
- A2. The number of weeks each cohort occupies is reported; `fewBlocks` also fires if either cohort occupies fewer than 5 weeks (for the procedures where weeks are the unit; for the moving blocks the equivalent is span D < 10 L or either cohort occupying fewer than 5 distinct blocks' worth of days, i.e. fewer than 5 L days).
- A3. Verdict (replaces the rule above): `unchanged` iff for every band and every procedure that has an interval the includes-zero reading equals the joint-day (M491) reading; `depends on the resampling unit` if any differs; `not assessable` for a band in which every procedure is fewBlocks; the overall verdict is the worst band (depends > not assessable > unchanged).
- A4. In-tool block-length-1 reference: the moving-block routine is also run with L = 1 (circular) on the same stream; width ratios are reported against both the M491 joint-day interval and this reference, so the block effect is separated from stream noise (diagnostic only).
- A5. Known-answer tests on a realistic layout (cohorts segregated in time, about 25 % shared dates, empty days). KAT6: iid segregated data: the W / WS inflation over the day design is reported and bounded (mean over 20 seeds, W/day <= 1.5). KAT7: the circular moving-block bootstrap distribution has its mean within Monte Carlo error of the point estimate (the non-circular rule fails this on the same layout, shown as a negative control). KAT3 (L = 1 equals the day bootstrap) holds only with no empty days; stated and tested on such data.
- A6. Blind audit E also reports week counts per cohort, drop counts and checks the circular rule.
Risks recorded by the Director: week blocks also absorb season and composition variance, not only serial dependence; with few weeks the percentile interval undercovers; all M491 readings include zero, so a flip would mean a narrowed or shifted interval, most likely an artefact. Wording: 'block sensitivity across neighbouring dates; uncertainty analysis, not a change of the point estimate'; 'thermal cohorts (ambient threshold), confounded with season, date and drift'; never robust / confirmed / replicated / no seasonal effect / accounts for dependence.
