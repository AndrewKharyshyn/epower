## 1. Estimand

Apparent battery-side KE recovery of a group G of eligible deceleration intervals: `100 * sum(charge_kwh) / sum(ke_kwh)` over the SAME intervals (ratio of sums; never a mean of interval percentages; not clipped to 100 %). `ke_kwh = 0.5 m (v0^2 - v1^2) / 3.6e6` (speeds in km/h converted to m/s inside) with the class-conditional assumed mass of the legacy (`_mass_for_drive`); `charge_kwh` = the charge-direction (battery-terminal) energy over the same interval. It does not identify motor/inverter efficiency, friction-brake energy, a BMS acceptance ceiling or battery health. "Apparent", "estimated", assumed mass; never "measured".

## 2. Interval definition (v2)

- **Speed intervals.** The resolved speed channel (`_apply_speed_priority`, M167 rule) provides NATIVE samples: raw rows with a numeric value inside 0-260 km/h. An interval is the span between two consecutive native speed samples `[t_k, t_{k+1}]`. Its duration is the actual `dt = t_{k+1} - t_k`; intervals with `dt > 5 s` or `dt <= 0` are gaps (excluded, counted `speedGap`); they are never bridged and never assigned a fixed 1 s duration. KE loss must be positive (`v_k > v_{k+1}`); the end speed may be 0 (a stop is a valid deceleration end; legacy dropped it by the `sp > 0` rule) and such intervals are flagged `endsAtStandstill`.
- **Charge energy.** Current and voltage are interpolated linearly between their NATIVE samples that bracket the interval, only if both bracketing samples exist and are at most 5 s apart; otherwise the interval is excluded (`currentOrVoltageUnsupported`). `P(t) = (-I(t)) V(t) / 1000` kW (BMS current is charge-positive, so `P < 0` is charging); `charge_kwh = integral of max(-P, 0) dt / 3600` evaluated on a 20 Hz sub-grid with the trapezoid rule so that the positive portion is integrated correctly across zero crossings. An interval with no charging has `charge_kwh = 0` exactly and stays in the denominator. The same value with `I + i_off` (`i_off` = `offsetUncertainty.pointEstimateA`, discharge-positive frame, read at run time) is stored as `chargeOffsetKwh` for the mandatory dual report; the point basis stays the declared uncorrected current (M440 ruling: the offset is coupled to `CAP_KWH`, verified:false, and not confirmable from rest periods).
- **Engine state.** Engine-off = RPM known and `<= 300 rpm` (owner decision (g): the proxy is kept) at BOTH interval ends, using the latest native RPM sample at or before the end with age `<= 5 s`, and no native RPM sample inside the interval above 300. An interval with unknown RPM at either end is excluded (`rpmUnknown`); unknown is never engine-off. Engine-on intervals are excluded and counted (`engineOn`).
- **Pack temperature.** The mean of T1-T4 at the interval START, each probe the latest native sample at or before `t_k` with age `<= 10 s`; all four probes are required (the full-probe rule); otherwise `tempUnsupported` (the interval stays in the speed and SOC views, not in the temperature view). Ambient temperature is not used anywhere in the estimator or the eligibility; a missing ambient never excludes an interval.
- **SOC.** The latest native SoC sample at or before `t_k` with age `<= 10 s` and inside 0-100 % (the M390 single-sample glitch handling is applied by the frame loader: `despike_frame`; its rejected count is reported); the SOC at the interval START describes the state preceding the interval's charging. Missing SOC (`socUnsupported`) creates a joint-support exclusion for SOC-conditioned views only.
- **Binning.** Primary speed variable = the interval START speed (the axis reads "speed at start of braking"); the END-speed binning of the legacy is kept as a secondary table so that legacy versus v2 isolates the integration change from the binning change. Speed bins: the published 10 km/h mid-bins (5, 15, ..., 125). Intervals ending below 10 km/h (the fade-out) are also reported as a separate row set, never mixed with the sub-10 km/h START bin. Temperature bins: `REGEN_TEMP_BINS` unchanged (absent bins stay explicit). SOC bands: edges `[40, 50, 55, 60, 63, 66, 69, 72, 100]` with lower-inclusive/upper-exclusive bands and the last band `[72, 100]` inclusive of 100; SOC < 40 is an explicit outside-band row ("below 40 %"); a SOC value outside 0-100 is invalid. These are display/analysis bands, not identified BMS thresholds.

## 3. Aggregation, uncertainty, support

- Sufficient statistics per drive and per calendar day: for every cell of (cohort) x (temperature bin x SOC selection) and (start-speed bin x SOC selection) and (end-speed bin x SOC selection): sum of charge, of offset-charge, of KE, of KE for urban/mixed (mass-range split), number of intervals, number with zero charge, seconds, drives and days. SOC selections: all SOC (joint-supported population), each of the 8 bands, below 40 %; plus the no-SOC-condition baseline of each view.
- **Observed estimate** per cell: ratio of sums; day-clustered percentile bootstrap (calendar day, B = 4000, seed 42, one set of resamples per cohort shared by all cells; numerator and denominator resampled together); the assumed-mass scenario range is computed in closed form as in the legacy (`_mass_uncertainty_bounds`) and kept apart from the statistical interval; the offset dual report is a second point value with the same support.
- **Support status** (global, declared now; operational reporting rules, not universal guarantees): `empty` (0 intervals); `thin` (fewer than 200 intervals OR fewer than 10 drives OR fewer than 5 calendar days: shown as exploratory, never certified); `supported` (all three floors met); `failed` (more than 2.5 % of bootstrap draws with a zero denominator: interval not available). The interval half-width is reported as a precision field, not a gate.
- **Joint-support impact.** For the temperature view the report states the intervals, KE and drives lost by requiring SOC versus the temperature-only baseline; nothing is imputed.
- **Reconciliation.** For identical support, the sum over SOC bands plus below-40 plus the missing-SOC part equals the temperature-only baseline exactly (intervals, KE, charge), and the all-SOC cell equals the sum of the band cells (G3 below).

## 4. Legacy comparison (reproduction anchors, not targets)

The v2 totals are reported beside the legacy's (M440 reproduction anchor: 123,076 steps, the published bins): eligible intervals, drives, days, KE and charge totals with the exclusion accounting by reason (`speedGap`, `currentOrVoltageUnsupported`, `engineOn`, `rpmUnknown`, `tempUnsupported`, `socUnsupported`, `endsAtStandstill` kept), and the per-bin legacy versus v2 (end-speed binned) versus v2 (start-speed binned) ratios. Differences are expected (interval integration, unknown RPM, offset reported separately) and are explained, not tuned.

## 5. Cache, orchestration, performance

- Per-drive cache (owner decision (f)): `regen_cache/<file>.npz` (git-ignored) holding the compact interval arrays of one drive (start time, dt, v0, v1, charge, offset-charge, KE, probe temperature, SOC at start, SOC age, class) plus a header with the raw source `src_md5` from the frame-cache manifest, the extractor version and the policy hash (declared constants of section 2). A cached drive is reused only if all three match; a changed raw byte, constant or bin policy invalidates that drive's entry (KAT). Aggregation reads the arrays only; no raw CSV is reread for an aggregation, a view change or a SOC selection.
- Stage `regen_v2` in `tools/ingest_stages.json` (after `thermal_support_ledger`): extracts only new or invalidated drives, aggregates all cohorts and splices the additive keys `regenCaptureV2` (the cells above, per cohort) and its artifact stamp; the legacy keys stay untouched in MC. First build and incremental times are measured and reported (target: seconds incremental, minutes first).
- Cohorts: all, warm, shoulder (thermal-regime split of `seasonal_drive_master.csv`) and cold (11 drives; exploratory, shown with the support status), by the same rules as the other additive blocks.

## 6. Gates and tests

- G1 (anchor): the M440 tool reproduces the published legacy bins before any v2 number is read (re-run, unchanged).
- G2 (isolation): the deep diff of `summary_arrays.json` shows only `regenCaptureV2` and its stamp.
- G3 (reconciliation): the sums of SOC-band cells + below-40 + missing-SOC equal the temperature-only baseline exactly; all-SOC equals the band sum; second run byte-identical; cached versus fresh extraction and worker counts 1 / 3 / 5 agree (float64 sums to 1e-9).
- Known-answer tests (criteria declared here, `tests/synthetic/test_regen_v2.py`): (1) a drive with a known speed drop, mass and current/voltage waveform reproduces the hand-calculated KE and charge energy, including a zero crossing; (2) identical temperature and braking at two SOC values land in two distinct bands and are selectable, with no monotonic rule; (3) a valid zero-charge deceleration lowers the pooled ratio through its KE; (4) missing or stale RPM, SOC, speed, current, voltage or probes produce the declared exclusion and never a fabricated zero or state; (5) a timestamp gap is not turned into a 1 s interval and never duplicates energy; (6) SOC and temperature boundary values (including 100 and the final edge) and values outside the legacy range follow the rules exactly once; (7) a deceleration spanning several native speed samples is several atomic intervals whose KE and charge sums equal the whole, each assigned its own SOC and temperature; (8) pooled recovery is a ratio of sums on unequal-KE intervals; (9) the joint-cell sums reconcile to all-SOC, selected-SOC and cohort totals; (10) CADENCE INVARIANCE: the same synthetic trajectory sampled every 1.0 s and every 1.4 s (and 2.6 s) gives the same ratio within the declared tolerance (the legacy fixed-1 s rule fails this test, shown in the test as the contrast); (11) whole-day resampling keeps all within-day contributions and paired sums; fixed seeds reproduce; (12) a drive warming through temperature bins at constant (or absent) ambient updates the bins and never changes eligibility; (13) cached versus fresh and changed raw bytes / policy invalidate the entry.
- Blind audit (analytical-auditor, claim + data path only): reproduce the interval counts by exclusion reason and, for the cohort all, the totals and the per-bin ratios (temperature all-SOC, start-speed all-SOC and two SOC bands) from the raw files and section 2; Director ruling before any display.

## 7. Wording and decision rules (declared before any result)

"Apparent KE recovery (assumed mass)", "estimated", "interval-based extractor v2", never "measured", "corrected" or "true". ## 8. Limits (disclosed in advance)

Single vehicle, one driver, assumed masses, May-October (the cold cohort has 11 drives on 4 days); the native speed resolution is 1 km/h (low-speed intervals are dominated by quantization); the 20 Hz sub-grid integration is an approximation of the piecewise-bilinear power (error bound tested); SOC and temperature are attributes at the interval START (a long interval changes them within; atomic intervals are short); the offset is a sensitivity (dual report); the 300 rpm engine-off proxy is a declared convention; unmatched speed-versus-I/V timing (the speed PID and the BMS PIDs are polled asynchronously) is handled by interpolating I and V to the interval ends, not by modelling latency.

## 9. Rev 2 (2026-10-15): Director review of Rev 1 (revise) adopted; this section supersedes sections 2-6 where they conflict

- **B1 Episodes (plateaus).** A strict-drop-only rule would discard equal-speed intervals inside a deceleration although their charge is real, and their share grows with polling rate and 1 km/h quantization. An *episode* is a maximal run of consecutive valid engine-off intervals with `v_{k+1} <= v_k` that starts and ends with a strict drop (leading and trailing plateaus are trimmed). Inner plateau intervals enter with KE 0 and their charge; the numbers of kept and of edge-dropped plateau intervals are reported. Any invalid interval, a gap, an engine-on interval or a speed increase ends the episode.
- **B2 Speed bins by instantaneous speed.** One slow-cadence interval spans 10-15 km/h, so binning a whole interval by its start (or end) speed depends on the polling. Primary: the speed within an interval is taken as linear between its two native samples; the interval is cut into 0.05 s slices; the KE loss of a slice (exact, telescoping: the slices sum to the interval KE) and its charge go to the 10 km/h bin of the slice's mid-point speed. The START-speed and END-speed tables of the interval remain as the legacy bridge. The axis reads "speed during braking (interpolated between speed samples)", never "start of braking". Temperature and SOC are interval-START attributes applied to every slice of the interval (their sums are not split).
- **B3 Disjoint interval classes** (diagnostic partition, all views include all of them in the primary tables): A = start speed < 10 km/h; B = start >= 10 and end < 10 (threshold crossing, includes `endsAtStandstill`); C = end >= 10. Secondary tables restrict to start >= 10 km/h. With the slice rule the sub-10 km/h speed bin is the braking while the speed is below 10 km/h; classes A, B, C are reported for the totals and per temperature bin.
- **B4 Additions.** Every view and the cadence mix per temperature bin are also reported by cadence regime (master `ev_rpm_dt_med_s` below 1.0 s = fast, otherwise slow; missing = unknown); a sensitivity with a 2 s ceiling on `dt` and on the I/V bracket gap; the speed-zone regrouping (`regenByZone` v2: the zone edges 30 / 60 / 90 / 120 coincide with 10 km/h bin edges, so the zones are sums of the bin sums, with the same bootstrap) is INCLUDED, resolving M440 clarification C4.
- **B5 KAT tolerance and realism (declared now).** KAT (10) is split: (10a) exact case: piecewise-linear speed and current with knots on both sampling grids and no zero crossing, sampled at 1.0, 1.4 and 2.6 s: totals and per-cell ratios equal within 1e-9 relative; (10b) realistic case: a smooth deceleration with 1 km/h speed quantization, current quantization and jittered `dt` (seed 42): totals and temperature / SOC / speed-bin cells agree between the three cadences within 0.5 percentage points (the declared bound from the interpolation error), and the legacy rule on the same trajectories misses by at least 5 times that tolerance (>= 2.5 pp on the totals).
- **B6 Real-data cadence-invariance study.** Fast-cadence drives (ev_rpm_dt_med_s < 1.0 s) are re-run with alternate whole rows dropped; per view total and per bin, the paired difference (full minus decimated) with a day-clustered 95 % interval is reported. Decision rule: if the interval excludes zero AND |difference| exceeds the half-width of the bin's mass-uncertainty range, v2 is not cadence-invariant in that cell; if this holds for any supported cell of a primary view, stage ME is blocked until the Director rules.
- **B7 Cache key and content.** The key contains the SHA-256 of the raw file bytes, the versions of the loader (frame-cache schema), the despike step and the speed-priority rule, the policy hash of section 2 and Rev 2 constants, and the `i_off` value used for the offset-charge column (the offset charge is nonlinear in it). Mass-free quantities are stored (`dv2 = v0^2 - v1^2`, per slice and per interval) and the class mass and the cohort are applied at aggregation.
- **Clarifications.** Population: every master row with a frame (as the legacy; the ens-clean subset is a reported sensitivity). Ordered bridge from the legacy to v2 (each step reported): legacy; + RPM known / consecutive rows / full probes (the MB fixes); + interval integration; + standstill ends; + plateaus; + instantaneous-speed binning. The SOC channel is the BMS SoC (`soc`), its resolution is reported; duplicate timestamps keep the last value; I and V are piecewise-linear through EVERY native sample with every bracketing gap <= 5 s; a speed-versus-BMS lag sensitivity shifts the I/V times by +1 s and -1 s; the cold cohort has 4 days, so all its cells are thin by construction; `release_check` checks the stamp only; the audit tolerance is exact for counts and 0.1 pp for ratios; a cell above 100 % is flagged and never clipped.

## 10. Rev 2.1 (2026-10-15): Director confirmation review of Rev 2 ("revise": R1-R6 and follow-ups adopted verbatim in substance; sections 9 and 10 supersede earlier text)

Finding behind R1: the raw rows are sparse (one or a few PIDs per row, about 60 ms apart), so dropping alternate whole ROWS does not halve each channel's cadence; depending on the poll-cycle parity it can delete a PID entirely or leave it at full rate.

- **R1 (B6 decimation per channel).** Decimate per channel: keep every 2nd NATIVE sample of speed, I, V, RPM, SoC and T1-T4 (not whole rows). Both phases (even / odd) are run and reported separately; the decision uses their mean. Sub-sampling happens on the native arrays inside the extractor (`decimate=phase`).
- **R2 (B1 episodes).** Only `speedGap`, `currentOrVoltageUnsupported`, `rpmUnknown`, `engineOn` and a speed increase end an episode. Temperature or SOC support never breaks or trims an episode (keeps the G3 reconciliation). The charge and the seconds of the trimmed leading / trailing plateaus are reported (diagnostic, not in the estimator).
- **R3 (B2 slices).** `n = ceil(dt / 0.05 s)` equal slices; the charge trapezoid uses the same nodes; interval charge = sum of the slice charges. Speed bins are `[10 j, 10 j + 10)`; slices with mid-point speed >= 130 km/h go to an explicit overflow row (`>=130`), included in the zone `>120`.
- **R4 (counting).** An interval counts in every speed bin it touches (counts are therefore not additive across speed bins; sums of KE and charge are). Support floors (n >= 200, >= 10 drives, >= 5 days) count STRICT-DROP intervals only (plateau intervals carry charge, not events).
- **R5 (KAT 10a).** 1e-9 holds only with constant V and bin-edge crossings on nodes shared by every cadence's sub-grid; otherwise the bound is one slice per edge crossing (stated in the test).
- **R6 (B6 study).** Unit = ratio difference in percentage points; fast-cadence drives only, cohort all; the supported status is taken from the full-run fast subset. Decision views: temperature all-SOC, speed all-SOC, SOC-band totals; other cells are reported only. A common-support variant (both runs restricted to intervals eligible in the decimated run) separates eligibility from integration.
- **Follow-ups.** Cache key adds the extractor SOURCE hash and a variant id (lag, 2 s ceiling, decimation phase); the offset charge (nonlinear in `i_off`) is stored in a SEPARATE file per drive keyed by `i_off`, so an offset update does not invalidate the main arrays. The extractor stores per-drive median `dt` of speed and of I/V; the cadence mix is given as KE share and interval share per temperature bin and SOC band. SOC outside 0-100 is dropped before the latest-sample lookup.
