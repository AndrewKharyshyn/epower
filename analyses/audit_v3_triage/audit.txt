Fuel tab implementation addendum
Deep project audit - version 3
Implementation recommendations | User decision recorded 1 October 2026
Place all fuel-related charts in one dedicated top-level Fuel tab. This includes the existing fuel-consumption
and fuel-derived reconstruction views. Other tabs may link to the corresponding Fuel section. The
measured HV-bus architecture stays in Energy with a link to the model-derived accounting in Fuel.
This revision adds a specification for implementation. It does not certify that the charts, computations or
dashboard changes have been implemented. The complete 73-page version 2 audit follows unchanged,
including its 14-page continuation and original 59-page report. Where navigation recommendations differ,
this addendum supplies the updated Fuel-tab placement.
Delivery phase
Scope
Foundation
Repair the fuel data contract, eligible masks, model residuals and seasonal routing before
publishing new claims.
First release
Fuel overview, migration of existing views, charts FUEL-01 to FUEL-05, and the FUEL-12 quality
view.
Further analysis
Operating maps, stopping/EV relationships, start profiles, supported cohort comparisons and
distributions.
Optional views
Fuel cost and fuel Records with explicit qualification. Model-derived accounting remains
conditional on its repairs.

3. Calculations and scientific safeguards
 Metric / rule
Definition and requirement
Fuel from counter
F_counter = C(t_end) - C(t_start), over a reset-free, supported interval. Sum only
explicitly identified valid segments after a reset; report the covered segments rather
than silently calling this a full trip.
Fuel from rate
F_flow = integral(q(t) dt) / 3600 when q is L/h and timestamps are seconds. Declare
interpolation, maximum bridge gap and boundary handling. Retain unknown fuel across
excluded gaps.
Trip / group consumption
C_trip = 100 F / D. C_group = 100 sum(F_i) / sum(D_i), on eligible shared intervals. The
group value is distance-weighted consumption.
Matched denominators
Distance must cover the same endpoints as fuel. A common-clock fuel delta cannot be
divided by an unrelated full-trip distance. Validate distance counters or integrate
supported speed.
Stationary state
Use a declared, versioned speed threshold. Classify missing or stale speed as unknown.
Treat thresholds and dwell rules as sensitivity choices.
Fuel-state allocation
Sum integrated fuel by supported temporal state. State totals plus unknown must
reconcile to the same-clock fuel integral, not an unmatched trip counter.
Near-neutral subset
Show start/end SoC and a declared near-neutral window with sensitivity. Small SoC
change is a useful descriptive check, not proof of zero battery-energy change.
SoC correction scenario
Any correction needs a battery-energy relationship and a fuel-equivalent conversion
model. Mark assumed 2.1 kWh capacity and efficiency parameters as unverified; show
observed and corrected values separately. [F-R2]
Do not compute a group mean by averaging instantaneous consumption or unweighted trip rates. At zero
or very low speed, use L/h. Use L/100 km only where supported positive distance exists. A zero counter
increment is subject to its recorded grid and timing; label it "no recorded fuel increment" where that is
what the data show.
For uncertainty, resample logged dates jointly across cohorts; keep all trips from a date together. For predictive models,
hold out dates and fit preprocessing inside the training split. Distinguish trip variability, confidence in a mean, measurement
uncertainty and scenario sensitivity. Keep route, stop fraction, trip length, thermal state, HVAC and battery inventory as
potential confounders. [Original audit R7]

4. Trip length and warm-up
FUEL-01 | Fuel consumption versus trip length
Question. Are high trip consumption rates concentrated in short journeys, and how does starting engine
temperature relate to them?
Calculation. Use reset-checked trip fuel and aligned distance. Retain absolute litres and recomputed L/100
km for the same trip. Store start coolant/oil temperature, quality and SoC change. Any trend fit must
account for thermal state and repeated dates.
Presentation. Two aligned scatterplots: litres versus km and L/100 km versus km. Colour by temperature or
facet by temperature band. An optional logarithmic distance axis needs explicit ticks. Compare uses
aligned cohort panels with the same distance and rate scales.
Acceptance. Both panels reference identical eligible trip IDs. Tooltips include fuel, distance, temperature, date, SoC change
and source. Zero-distance journeys appear only in a duration/fuel view. A fitted intercept is not automatically fuel wasted at
startup.
FUEL-02 | Warm-up fuel trajectory
Question. How do cumulative fuel use and consumption evolve after departure or the first observed engine
start?
Calculation. Define the alignment event and temperature bands. Compute cumulative fuel on time and
distance axes and fuel/distance in successive supported moving intervals. Track stationary fuel separately.
Censor trajectories at trip end or unsupported gaps; show the number of contributing trips at each
position.
Presentation. Overlaid cumulative curves by initial coolant/oil condition, with interval-consumption and
support panels. Distinguish cold first starts from warm restarts. Compare uses the same alignment, bands
and window boundaries, plus optional matched-condition differences.
Acceptance. No zero-filled continuation after a trip ends. Longer trips cannot silently replace the short-trip population along
the curve. Missing initial temperature is labelled or excluded with a count. Describe associations unless the comparison
identifies a causal thermal penalty. [F-R3]
First-release scope
Extend the existing thermal-fuel builder instead of adding an independently maintained scalar penalty. The scalar, curves,
support and prose should be produced by the same analysis contract. Show both initial temperature and ambient cohort
because they describe different conditions.

5. Stationary fuel and charge balance
FUEL-03 | Fuel while stationary and moving
Question. What fraction and volume of recorded fuel use occurs during supported stops?
Calculation. Integrate native fuel rate over a common speed clock. Allocate to stationary, moving and
unknown speed. Preserve coverage and compare classification across declared speed/dwell thresholds.
Battery charging/discharging may be an optional sub-classification only where valid I/V exists.
Presentation. Paired stacked bars for litres and shares, grouped by trip-length band or driving class. Keep
state colours consistent across seasons. Compare groups cohort bars beside one another; use cohort
labels or borders rather than changing state colours.
Acceptance. All state fuel sums equal the shared-window integral within the declared numerical tolerance. Unknown is
visible. Use "stationary fuel" and "fuel consumed during net pack charging"; these are temporal states, not exclusive
fuel-source allocations or measured waste.
FUEL-04 | Fuel consumption versus SoC change
Question. Do lower-consumption trips finish with less stored battery charge, and higher-consumption trips
with more?
Calculation. Compute end-minus-start SoC on validated fuel/distance intervals. Plot observed trip
consumption first. Define the near-neutral subset and any modelled correction in the contract. Facet by
distance to avoid comparing a small charge change on a short trip with the same change on a long trip.
Presentation. Scatterplot: SoC change in percentage points versus L/100 km, with distance facets and a
zero-change reference. Compare uses common axes and a consistent near-neutral band. Add a separate
observed / near-neutral / correction-scenario summary.
Acceptance. The correction has an assumptions disclosure and sensitivity. Starting SoC is available in tooltips. A regression
on SoC change alone does not identify battery capacity, fuel-to-electric efficiency or the correct HEV correction factor.
[F-R2]
Extend SocBalancedFuel using the shared observed-fuel contract. A dashboard selection must never silently swap the
observed result for an assumed corrected result. Near-neutral and corrected results can have different populations and
must show their own support.

6. Trip trace and engine operating map
FUEL-05 | Detailed trip fuel timeline
Question. How do engine starts, fuel use and battery buffering relate during an individual journey?
Calculation. Keep each signal on its native timestamp and align only within a documented tolerance.
Expose fuel rate, cumulative fuel, speed, RPM, SoC, battery power and coolant/oil temperature where
available. Decimation should preserve event timing, extremes and integrated fuel.
Presentation. Vertically linked panels with one time axis and cursor. Use separate unit scales rather than
overlaying unrelated signals on multiple axes. Compare two explicitly selected trips side by side, with
elapsed-time or distance alignment; it is an example comparison, not a cohort estimate.
Acceptance. Missing I/V removes only the battery-power panel. Native gaps remain visible. The trip selector respects cohort
and quality filters. Cursor values disclose sample times/alignment. Cumulative litres reconcile to the same selected window.
FUEL-06 | Engine fuel-use operating map
Question. Which observed engine operating regions account for most operating time and fuel volume?
Calculation. Bin native RPM and an available engine-load signal using fixed shared boundaries. For each
cell, store time-weighted mean fuel rate, operating time, integrated litres, trips and dates. Preserve the
exact load PID definition and do not substitute fuel-derived horsepower.
Presentation. A fuel-rate heatmap accompanied by a support/occupancy heatmap. Optionally switch the
main statistic to fuel contribution, with a changed unit and legend. Compare uses side-by-side maps with
identical bins and colour limits; a difference map needs support in both cohorts.
Acceptance. No interpolation across unsupported cells. Sparse cells are marked and excluded from claims. Time weights
use elapsed time, not row count. This is a fuel-use map, not BSFC or thermal efficiency; those require independent
output-power information.
For e-POWER, vehicle speed alone is an incomplete description of engine fuelling because the battery buffers demand. The
linked trace and operating map should expose this relationship without claiming that fuel use directly measures generator
electrical power.

7. Stops, speed and electric-driving share
FUEL-07 | Fuel consumption versus stopping intensity
Question. How does journey stopping relate to consumption within comparable trip lengths?
Calculation. Compute supported stationary-time fraction, moving average speed and trip consumption.
Keep the speed denominator explicit: moving-time mean differs from distance divided by total trip time.
Use pooled fuel/distance for consumption bins and retain trip/day support.
Presentation. Scatterplot of stop fraction versus L/100 km with trip-length facets; a companion
moving-speed curve. In Compare, use shared panels or binned curves, and show the stopping/speed
distribution below the rate plot.
Acceptance. Missing speed cannot count as stationary. The same distance/thermal restrictions apply to both cohorts. Do not
describe a marginal speed curve as an aerodynamic or traffic effect without controlling the other journey conditions.
FUEL-08 | Fuel consumption versus electric-driving share
Question. Is a greater engine-off traction distance share associated with lower observed fuel use?
Calculation. Use the validated EV-distance definition from the existing project and pair it with the
same-window fuel/distance data. Preserve unknown RPM/state support. Store SoC change, start
temperature, trip length and date. Keep engine-off time share separate from EV-distance share.
Presentation. Scatterplot of EV-distance percentage versus L/100 km, coloured by SoC change and faceted
by trip length. Compare uses common panels, optionally with an independently declared near-neutral
subset. The zero-fuel label follows the recorded counter resolution.
Acceptance. Fuel and EV percentage use the same eligible interval or explicitly disclose the mismatch. Missing RPM cannot
become engine-off. Higher EV share does not prove free energy, a fuel saving attributable to regeneration, or improved
tank-to-wheel efficiency.
Interpretation
Use these charts to describe operational associations. A high EV share can follow earlier fuel-powered charging, while the
current trip may draw down stored battery energy. Trip length, temperature, traffic, route and SoC balance remain part of
the explanation.

8. Engine starts and supported cohorts
FUEL-09 | Engine-start fuel profiles
Question. How does observed fuelling following a cold first start differ from a warm restart?
Calculation. Reuse a validated start-event definition with RPM/fuel coverage. Define pre/post windows,
censor unsupported boundaries and reject stale joins. Integrate fuel in each supported window; store
temperature, event duration, trip context, SoC and date. Multiple events on one date are dependent.
Presentation. Event-aligned L/h profiles plus millilitres in declared post-start windows. Compare cold first
starts and warm restarts before splitting further by ambient cohort. Display contributing events, trips and
dates along the profile.
Acceptance. A fully supported window cannot be inferred from one sample. Missing pre-start context is labelled. Report
"fuel in the post-start window"; additional restart fuel needs a suitable counterfactual model. Series-HEV research motivates
this distinction. [F-R4]
FUEL-10 | Fuel comparisons under similar conditions
Question. Do observed cohort differences remain on shared journey and thermal support?
Calculation. Provide pooled observed rates first. Define common driving-class / distance / stopping /
initial-temperature support before adjusting. A standardized result uses declared fixed target weights and
stratum-specific fuel/distance ratios; it is a different target from the full cohort. Bootstrap dates jointly and
report unsupported draws.
Presentation. Dot-and-interval plots showing observed and standardized estimates with trips, dates and
km. Compare displays Warm, Shoulder and supported Cold with consistent units. Add an explicit difference
plot only for a well-defined shared target.
Acceptance. The current Cold population of two trips on one date supports description, not a general winter effect or an
inferential interval. Do not copy whole-corpus throughput contrasts into fuel charts. Do not treat a sparse-support gate as
proof of inferential adequacy.
Name the actual estimand in the chart title: observed pooled consumption, consumption on matched conditions, or a
standardized target. Adjustment cannot remove unmeasured route/HVAC confounding or create missing Cold observations.

9. Distributions, trends and fuel quality
FUEL-11 | Consumption distribution and chronological trend
Question. What is typical trip consumption, which journeys are unusual, and does the observed pattern
change over time?
Calculation. Use recomputed trip rates within declared distance groups. For ECDFs, expose trip weighting
versus distance weighting as separate statistics. For trends, compute a ratio of total fuel to total km over
declared calendar or distance windows and show the contributing exposure.
Presentation. ECDFs or well-supported distribution plots plus a rolling pooled-consumption panel. Compare
overlays ECDFs with cohort colour and line style. Calendar curves preserve actual dates; filtered missing
days remain gaps rather than a continuous seasonal story.
Acceptance. Do not sum percentages or average daily/trip L/100 km into a fleet rate. The date ordering and overlap of
windows are disclosed. Coverage and trip-composition changes are annotated. Sparse bins use individual points rather than
an unstable violin/density shape.
FUEL-12 | Fuel-data agreement and consistency
Question. Do logged rate, counter and matching average fields tell a consistent numerical story?
Calculation. Pair flow integration and counter deltas on the same endpoints. Plot difference versus their
mean; retain units, grid, reset/gap flags and input hash status. Validate the matching app average against
recomputed fuel/distance only after establishing its reset window and update timing.
Presentation. Difference-versus-mean plot, residual table, coverage summary and filterable flagged trips.
Compare uses aligned cohort panels with common limits or explicit cohort markers. Show relative
differences only when positive volumes are above a declared resolution floor.
Acceptance. The preserved audit case is 234 trips with +0.240% aggregate flow/counter difference; it is a regression
reference for that frozen data version, not a universal acceptance tolerance. Agreement does not establish independence or
external accuracy. [Original audit R9]
The quality view should distinguish measurement provenance, numerical integration sensitivity, logger consistency and
model validity. A very high correlation does not validate a fuel meter or the reconstructed generator split.

10. Costs, fuel Records and reconstruction
FUEL-13 | Fuel cost - optional
Question. What do the recorded journeys cost at a declared fuel price?
Calculation. Compute cost = fuel litres x price per litre; compute cost per 100 km from total cost and total
eligible distance. Use explicit dated prices or a clearly labelled constant-price scenario. Validate existing
app-cost fields independently of the new display.
Presentation. Litres and cost per trip or distance group, with the fuel price and currency visible. Compare
at one common price for economy comparisons; a separate dated-price view can describe expenditure.
Acceptance. Changing price updates cost only, not fuel consumption. Missing price remains unavailable. Existing app price
changes must not be interpreted as vehicle efficiency changes.
FUEL-14 | Qualified fuel Records - optional
Question. Which journeys and sustained events are genuine extremes under the stated metric?
Calculation. Track largest litres/trip; highest and lowest L/100 km within declared distance and
near-neutral groups; largest stationary volume/share; and highest supported rolling fuel rate. Define rolling
duration, completeness and contiguous-window rules. Keep no-increment journeys separate.
Presentation. Ranked point/range charts and a Records table in Fuel. Other Records pages can link here.
Compare applies the same qualification to each cohort and retains tied holders.
Acceptance. Minimum-distance and SoC rules are versioned sensitivity choices. Native peaks differ from rolling peaks. Each
holder has file, timestamp/window, numerator, denominator, temperature, SoC change, quality and ties; missing or
quantized fuel is not a perfect-economy record.
Existing generator-to-traction reconstruction
Retain a model accounting table with source boundaries, fuel basis, assumptions, eligible mask and residual. The audit
found 15.94 kWh/100 km modelled generator output versus 16.99 in displayed outgoing branches, an excess of 1.05
kWh/100 km. Repair the common-mask node ledger before a conserved Sankey or definitive allocation claim. Extend the
warning to Compare. Moving the view to Fuel does not resolve original finding F04.
Do not add fuel-to-wheel efficiency, BSFC, generator efficiency or litres saved by regeneration as measured charts from
these fuel channels alone. Such outputs need independent energy/power information and, for savings, a defensible
counterfactual. Any optional CO2 conversion needs a documented fuel-specific factor and a tailpipe-only boundary.

12. Seasonal prose and unavailable data
Season/cohort classification must come from the validated, versioned thermal policy and its source field,
not month names. Expose whether ambient temperature was recorded or filled. Initial engine temperature
and ambient cohort are separate variables; a Warm-cohort journey may still begin with a cool engine.
Situation
Required displayed narrative / behaviour
Supported observed rate
"Recorded fuel consumption was {rate} L/100 km over {km} km, from {trips} trips on
{dates} dates." Values share one contract.
Partial signal coverage
State the covered interval and excluded distance/time. Update both the chart and
paragraph. Do not present a partial interval as a complete trip.
Cold available but thin
Report the actual trips/dates and descriptive values. State that the cohort is
insufficient for a general winter comparison; do not say no Cold data exists.
Zero eligible observations
Show unavailable with the reason and missing prerequisite. Remove stale curves,
values and comparative conclusions.
Stationary fuel result
Use "{litres} L was recorded while speed was classified as stationary." If battery state
is present, describe the simultaneous state without assigning all fuel to charging.
Charge correction scenario
Report observed and scenario-corrected results with population and assumptions.
State that correction uncertainty is model-dependent.
Cohort difference
Name observed or standardized target, unit, common support and uncertainty. Treat
unsupported comparisons as unavailable.
Uncertain sign or negligible effect
Use a factual estimate/interval statement. A confidence interval containing zero is not
proof of equal consumption.
Model-derived accounting
Keep model assumptions, residual and identification limits next to the figure in All,
cohort and Compare views.
Dynamic output contract
Generate numbers, date spans, qualifiers and conclusion sentences from the selected chart view-model.
Template wording may be static; empirical assertions such as only urban, no highway, no Cold, majority,
warmer is better, or a precise penalty must be conditional on computed evidence. Rounding is a
presentation operation and must not change thresholds, ranks or significance decisions.
Changing a global filter must update summary cards, traces, Records, method footnotes, support strips, downloadable
tables, titles and every internal chart switch. Old All-mode payloads must never survive behind a cohort heading.

14. Scientific use and references
Article opportunities using the existing dataset
1. Short-trip fuel burden and thermal state: model absolute trip litres using distance, initial coolant/oil
temperature, stationary time and battery inventory covariates. Compare predictive performance on
held-out dates and report the remaining route/HVAC confounding. A fitted startup term is an association,

What a coarse grid means for a spread
If each channel was rounded to nearest with a known step q, the individual rounding error is bounded
by q/2 and the spread error by (q_max + q_min)/2. With both steps at 100 mV that bound is +/-100 mV.
Independent uniform rounding errors would give a standard uncertainty of sqrt(q_max^2 +
q_min^2)/sqrt(12), or 40.8 mV. Those distribution/independence assumptions are scenarios, not facts
about these files. Correlated errors, min/max selection, asynchronous polling and temporal dependence
require additional treatment [N1-N2].
Rounding cannot recover sub-grid voltage differences. Random jitter is not a restoration of the
originals; it would add assumptions to the health response. Preserve the files, label the quality issue,
and request the original unrounded channel exports.

Common definitions and denominator discipline
With discharge-positive power P(t)=-Iraw(t)V(t)/1000, Eout=integral max(P,0)dt and Ein=integral
max(-P,0)dt, in kWh. Gross throughput is Eout+Ein; net draw is Eout-Ein. Under nominal energy Q=2.1
kWh, GTC=(Eout+Ein)/Q and FCE=(Eout+Ein)/(2Q). Q is an assumption in this dataset, not an
independently measured usable capacity.
A pooled per-distance quantity is 100 × sum(E)/sum(distance) on the same eligible rows. It is not the
arithmetic mean of trip-specific rates, and unavailable-energy kilometres must not appear in its
denominator. A per-drive mean remains a valid different statistic if labelled accordingly.

A method is not outdated merely because the original paper is old. Numerical integration, rainflow and
transparent clustered regression remain appropriate tools. The parts to retire are unsupported transfers,
outdated empirical facts, mislabeled observables and confidence claims that exceed what the sensors
and design identify.
What the existing data cannot identify
• Absolute capacity SOH, loss of lithium inventory or active material, plating occurrence, and
pack-specific cycle/calendar life without suitable reference tests and calibration.
• The full physical generator-to-traction direct/buffered split, motor/inverter/wheel efficiency or a
complete BSFC surface without simultaneous branch/input-output power.
• Precise high-frequency DCIR from asynchronous low-rate current/voltage polling, even if the regression
fit has many rows.
• Separate intrinsic heat capacity and cooling conductance from a one-pole thermal response with
unknown heat input, airflow/HVAC and parking history.
• Whole-year winter consumption or winter ageing from two short Cold drives on one date; fleet-wide
AWD/FWD effects from the small segregated auxiliary rail.
• A causal controller reason from RPM/SoC/boost association, or the cause of an observed speed/run
boundary from a classifier mask ending.
