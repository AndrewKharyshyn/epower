## Problem (read from the M614 replay and audit; script-written)
- Replay on whole-calendar-day prefixes (`analyses/M614_replay.json`, statsmodels 0.15.0, scipy 1.17.1): the shipped fit (`lbfgs`) raised `LinAlgError('Singular matrix')` at 18 of 21 grid points and fitted at 3; historic prefixes (drives, days, fitted): {'289': (168, 58, False), '632': (280, 103, False), '665': (294, 107, True)}.
- The auditor refitted the same prefixes with a fallback chain (bfgs, lbfgs, nm): all 21 of 21 points converged and none was singular; the largest signed difference between the mixed and the OLS slope over points with at least 30 days is 0.17 OLS standard errors.
- Consequence today: the sensitivity block is blank ("did not complete") at most corpus sizes although a converged fit exists; whether `valid` is true depends on one optimiser run, which is the property the M614 headline rule removed from the headline but left in the sensitivity.
- Current state: resistanceVreg mixed fit valid = True (converged True); cellSpread mixed fit valid = False (fitted False; `LinAlgError('Singular matrix')`).

## Question (one only)
Does a fixed, pre-registered multi-solver procedure give a mixed-effects sensitivity fit whose fixed-effect slope and day variance do not depend on which solver converged, so that the sensitivity can be reported at every corpus size instead of at the few where one optimiser run happens to complete?

## Options (the Director ranks; this spec pre-registers option B)
A. Leave as is (single `lbfgs`, honest "did not complete"): the status quo; correct but mostly empty.
B. Multi-solver procedure with an agreement check (this spec).
C. A different model family or Kenward-Roger / Satterthwaite degrees of freedom: a separate, larger task; out of scope (M614 spec section 9).
Out of scope: changing the headline, the TOST, the estimand, the covariates, the other mixed model of the repo (`interDriveCarryover.regressionModel.mixedEffects`, which has its own fallback chain, M388b); any refit of M119-v2.

## Pre-registered design
- Procedure for each trend metric (`resistanceVreg`, `cellSpread`; identical formula, random intercept per calendar day, REML, as `_mixedlm` today): fit with EVERY solver of the fixed list in the fixed order [lbfgs, bfgs, nm] (gradient-based quasi-Newton methods first, the derivative-free Nelder-Mead last; `lbfgs` first because it is the shipped solver, so a corpus on which it converges reproduces the shipped numbers). The order is fixed now by this principle and is not chosen from the replay.
- A solver result COUNTS only if the fit returned (no exception), the optimiser reports convergence, the fixed-effect SE is finite and positive, and its REML log-likelihood is within a relative 1e-6 of the best log-likelihood among the solvers that returned (a solver that stopped at a visibly worse objective has not reached the optimum and does not count).
- Reported fit: the first COUNTING solver in the fixed order. If no solver counts the block says so (as today) and lists each solver's outcome.
- Agreement: when at least two solvers count, the slope difference between the two most different counting solvers must be at most 0.05 OLS standard errors and their day variances must agree within a relative 5 % ("agree"); otherwise `solverAgreement` is "disagree". The tolerances were fixed before any per-solver table existed, but AFTER the M614 audit aggregate was seen (lbfgs against the fallback chain: largest signed mixed-minus-OLS difference 0.17046 against 0.17040 OLS standard errors, all 21 prefixes converged), so they are NOT blind; they are not tuned to any per-solver result and the replay may veto the procedure (below), never retune them. With exactly one counting solver the agreement is "single solver" (reported, not an agreement).
- `valid` (mixed-effects sensitivity) = a counting solver exists AND seNotBlownUp AND NOT reCovarianceSingular AND agreement is not "disagree".
- Additive payload fields under `degradationTrends.<metric>.mixedEffects`: `solver` (the reported solver or null), `solversTried` (per solver: counting yes or no, converged, error text, REML log-likelihood, slope, SE, group variance), `solverAgreement` ("agree" | "disagree" | "single solver" | null) with `maxSlopeDiffInOLSse` and `maxGroupVarRelDiff`. Existing keys (`slope`, `se`, `ci95`, `converged`, `groupVar`, `residVar`, `valid`, `validity`, ...) describe the reported solver; where the shipped single-solver fit fails and another solver counts, these values CHANGE from blank to a fit. The headline, the OLS block, the TOST fields and `headlineVsMixed` semantics are unchanged (`headlineVsMixed` is filled where it was null).
- Replay (descriptive, declared now, never used to choose or tune): the whole-day prefix grid of `analyses/M614_replay.py` (eligible counts 100, 110, ... and the historic 289-, 632-, 665-drive corpora), per metric and prefix: each solver's outcome, the reported solver, the agreement, the slope difference to the OLS slope in OLS standard errors; counts of "agree", "disagree", "single solver", "none". Library versions recorded. VETO (R9-style): if at the current corpus or at more than 10 % of the prefixes with at least 30 eligible days the agreement is "disagree", the mixed fit is solver-dependent and the procedure is not shipped (reported as such; the status quo A stands). Also reported: the largest absolute signed difference of the reported mixed slope from the OLS slope over prefixes with at least 30 days, against one OLS standard error (the M614 divergence criterion).
- Known-answer tests (written before the code; real solver convergence is optimiser and version dependent, so they inject the solver results): the procedure picks the first counting solver in the fixed order; an exception or a non-converged result does not count; a result with a clearly worse log-likelihood does not count; agree / disagree / single solver / none on constructed outcome sets (boundary cases at 0.05 OLS standard errors and 5 %); `valid` conjunction; the order [lbfgs, bfgs, nm] is exactly the declared one; the headline, OLS and TOST fields are byte-identical whatever the solvers do; on synthetic data with a planted day variance and a planted slope every counting solver recovers the slope within 4 standard errors. Real-corpus checks: a blind auditor reproduces each solver's outcome table on the grid from the rule and the data path only (tolerance 1e-3 relative on slopes and variances; convergence flags are reported as the auditor sees them, a difference is a finding, not a failure, because it is solver and version dependent).
- Payload and consumers: an additive leaf-level splice (`tools/m614_splice.py` pattern: recompute `degradationTrends`, freeze the OLS block, TOST and headline, allow-list only `mixedEffects.*` and `headlineVsMixed` leaves, derived evidence ledger unchanged); the estimator gate treats the changes as validity notes (informational, M615 R1); the dashboard text that binds to `mixedEffects` (the resistance sensitivity paragraph and the cell-spread validity sentence) follows the new values from S.*; a rendered before/after comparison shows which displayed sentences change and that no headline or OLS number moves (Dashboard change rule: render the dashboard and commit it with its sources); `language_gate.py` gets a rule against "unstable / singular" for a converged multi-solver fit.
- Accept / reject. Validity (failure = no splice): the known-answer tests; isolation diff (only the allow-listed leaves change; master MD5 unchanged); blind-audit reproduction as above; `release_check.py`, `validate_jsdom.js`, semantic gate. Scientific outcomes (reported, escalated, never tuned): agreement across solvers at the current corpus; the share of prefixes at which a fit exists; the divergence of the mixed slope from the OLS slope.
- Falsification, stated before any result: if solvers that all count give different slopes (more than 0.05 OLS standard errors) at the current corpus, then the mixed-effects slope is solver-dependent at this sample size and the premise that a multi-solver fit gives one answer is false; the result is reported and the status quo stays.
